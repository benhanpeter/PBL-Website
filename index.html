Bringing rail transit to the waterfront


A rail demonstration project to lay the groundwork for transit service on the Delaware River Waterfront.

Learn More

About

The Waterfront Trolley is a proposed one-mile battery-electric transit demonstration on Columbus Blvd. between Race St. and Queen St. It’s backed by a $1M Civic Innovation Grant from the National Science Foundation (NSF) and is being developed by a consortium of:
  • Pop-Up Metro
  • Delaware River Waterfront Corporation
  • Carnegie Mellon University
  • Philadelphia Belt Line (PBL) Railroad Company
The project will champion long-term transit service on the corridor by showing proof of concept of battery-electric and quick-build transit projects, and by practically demonstrating community demand for transit. It will be the first battery-electric transit system in the United States, revitalizing a line that has been dormant for nearly 30 years and reconnecting Philadelphia to the waterfront.

Proposed Service

The route of the demonstration will run along freight rail tracks owned by the Philadelphia Beltline Railroad, from the end of the line on Race St. to Pier 70, a distance of roughly one mile. Service will be operated on an “ad-hoc” basis; details about specific operating dates and times will be announced in advance.

History

The Philadelphia Belt Line Railroad (PBL) has been an active freight railroad since the late 1880s and is a staple of the Philadelphia rail industry by providing access for all rail operators to industrial sites along the Delaware River Waterfront. Today, the PBL is broken into two sections, PBL South and PBL North. The PBL South tracks end at Race St., at the Benjamin Franklin Bridge, and see minimal freight usage. This is the section that the Waterfront Line is planned to operate on.
In 1982, the Buckingham Valley Trolley Association established an electric heritage trolley service along PBL South. Ridership peaked in 1992 during Independence Day Celebrations at roughly 1,600 passengers. In 1996, the line was shut down due to Penn’s Landing Corporation requiring Pier 9, which was then used as car storage, for development. By 2000, all trolley equipment was relocated to Scranton, PA at the Electric City Trolley Museum. The project aspires to return service to the waterfront once again.

Public Engagement

The Waterfront Line is a project that has been and will continue to be brought to life with significant community involvement. In Winter 2025, a large public outreach campaign was conducted by Hinge Collective, a local architectural firm that specializes in community engagement. Tabling was done at local events, and an online survey was promoted, ultimately reaching over 4000 people. The purpose of the outreach was to gauge interest in passenger rail along the waterfront and gather feedback about how it would be most useful to the community. Over 80% of respondents indicated that the Waterfront Line would improve their experience when visiting the waterfront. We aim to reinforce these findings in the real world by operating the demonstration.

What's next?

The project team is currently working to prepare the right of way for a demo planned for 2027. These activities include trimming the trees along the tracks so as to not conflict with the train, construction of a light maintenance and charging facility, and track maintenance work. These activities will progress throughout 2026.
