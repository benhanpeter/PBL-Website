Bringing rail transit
to the waterfront

The Waterfront Line is a transit demonstration project which will bring passenger rail service to the Delaware River Waterfront along Columbus Blvd., from Race St. to Dockside.

About

The Waterfront Line is a proposed transit demonstration project on the Delaware River Waterfront. The project is being developed by the Waterfront Transit Initiative (WTI) a consortium of Pop Up Metro, Delaware River Waterfront Corporation, Carnegie Mellon University, and Philadelphia Belt Line Railroad Company as part of a $1 Million National Science Foundation Grant program called the Civic Innovation Program.

WTI intends to demonstrate a battery-electric transit service on Columbus Blvd. between Race St. and Queen St., a 1.01 mile segment. This demonstration would represent the first battery-electric transit system in the United States, revitalize a line that has been dormant for nearly 30 years, and help reconnect Philadelphia to the waterfront.

History

The Philadelphia Belt Line Railroad (PBL) has been an active freight railroad since the late 1880s and is a staple of the Philadelphia rail industry by providing access for all rail operators to industrial sites along the Delaware River Waterfront. Today, the PBL is broken into two section, PBL South and PBL North. The PBL South tracks end at Race St., at the Benjamin Franklin Bridge, and see very little freight usage. This is the section that WTI plans to operate on.

In 1982, the Buckingham Valley Trolley Association established an electric heritage trolley service along the PBLR South. Ridership peaked in 1992 during Independence Day Celebrations at roughly 1,600 passengers. In 1996, the line was shut down due to Penn’s Landing Corporation requiring Pier 9, which was then used as car storage, for development. By 2000, all trolley equipment was relocated to Scranton, PA at the Electric City Trolley Museum.

Proposed Service

WTI is proposing a demonstration along PBL South from the end of the line on Race St. to Pier 70 Blvd, a distance of roughly 1.01 miles. Sites have been identified for four platforms along the line:
  • Race St.
  • Penn’s Landing North
  • Penn’s Landing South
  • Dockside
The service will not have a fixed schedule but rather shuttle back and forth constantly throughout the day. Each trip will take roughly 12 minutes, at speeds under 20 mph.

Public Engagement

The Waterfront Line is a project that has been and will continue to be brought to life with significant community involvement. In Winter 2025, a large public outreach campaign was conducted by Hinge Collective, a local landscaping and architectural firm that specializes in community engagement. Tabling was done at local events, and an online survey was promoted, ultimately reaching over 4000 people. The purpose of the outreach was to gauge interest in passenger rail along the waterfront and gather feedback about how it would be most useful to the community.

What's next?

The project team is currently working to prepare the right of way for a demo planned for Fall 2026. These activities include trimming the trees along the tracks as to not conflict with the train, construction of a light maintenance and charging facility, and track maintenance work. These activities will progress throughout Summer 2026.
